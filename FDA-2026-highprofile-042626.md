10 High-Profile 510(k) Clearances (2025–2026)[1][2]
1. Apple: Hypertension Notification Feature (HTNF) for Apple Watch (K250507)
In a landmark clearance, the FDA granted Apple 510(k) status for an AI-powered algorithm designed to detect patterns indicative of hypertension using photoplethysmography (PPG) data.[1][2] By creating a new product code (SFR) specifically for machine learning-based cardiovascular notification software, the FDA signaled its willingness to formalize consumer-facing wearables as legitimate diagnostic-adjunct tools within the regulated medical space.[1][2]
2. Philips: Rembra Platform (Rembra CT, Rembra RT, Areta RT) (April 2026)
This multi-device clearance addresses the acute demand for high-throughput, precision imaging.[1][2][3] Featuring an 85 cm bore—the largest in its class—the Rembra platform is designed to streamline clinical workflows in emergency and cancer treatment settings.[1][2][3] The clearance reflects a strategic focus on integrating high-volume diagnostic capability with radiation therapy (RT) planning, allowing for more coordinated care.[1][2][3]
3. Philips: Spectral CT Verida System (April 2026)
Following the Rembra platform, the Verida system represents the next generation of spectral CT technology.[1][2] By combining spectral imaging—which provides tissue characterization data—with advanced AI reconstruction, this device enables clinicians to obtain higher clinical insight for oncology and cardiology workflows, reducing the need for repeat scans.[1][2]
4. GE HealthCare: True Definition DL Software (April 2026)
This software clearance underscores the transition from hardware-centric innovation to software-defined imaging.[1][2] The "True Definition" deep learning (DL) algorithms focus on reconstructing images to enhance clarity and reduce noise, effectively allowing existing CT hardware to perform at higher resolutions without requiring a complete system overhaul.[1][2]
5. Lincotek: SportLinc Syndesmosis Device (K252081, April 2026)
Representing advancements in orthopedic trauma care, the SportLinc device is a sterile, single-use implant designed for stabilizing high-ankle sprains.[1][2] Its inclusion of an ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) suture system tensioned between titanium buttons demonstrates the industry trend toward highly optimized, kit-based surgical solutions that are compatible with both hospital and ambulatory surgery center environments.[1][2]
6. Butterfly Network: Ultrasound AI Tool (March 2026)
Butterfly Network secured clearance for an AI-powered guidance tool for its portable ultrasound ecosystem.[1][2][4] This device demonstrates the continued democratization of imaging, using AI to bridge the skill gap for non-radiologists in point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS), specifically targeting gestational applications and rapid diagnostic assessment.[1][2]
7. MARS Bioimaging: Portable Photon-counting CT Scanner (March 2026)
This clearance represents a technical milestone in radiology.[1][2] Photon-counting CT offers superior resolution compared to conventional CT by detecting individual X-ray photons and their energy levels.[1][2] By achieving a portable form factor, this technology moves high-end diagnostic capability out of the radiology suite and closer to the patient’s bedside.[1][2]
8. Synova Life Sciences: Synova Wave Adipose Processing System (K251268, Nov 2025)
In the field of regenerative and plastic surgery, this device offers a specialized method for processing adipose (fat) tissue.[1][2] The clearance highlights the FDA's rigorous scrutiny of biologics-adjacent hardware, ensuring that the preparation of autologous tissue for surgical grafting meets safety standards for sterilization and cellular integrity.[1][2]
9. Roche: cobas® HIV-1/HIV-2 Qualitative Nucleic Acid Test (Nov 2025)
This clearance marks the continued evolution of high-throughput molecular diagnostics.[1][2] By optimizing these tests for the cobas® 5800/6800/8800 systems, Roche demonstrates the industry's focus on laboratory automation—enabling healthcare systems to process larger volumes of samples with faster turnaround times, critical for infectious disease management.[1][2]
10. MimiX Biotherapeutics: FastSkin Patch (Nov 2025)
This device represents an innovative approach to wound care and tissue engineering.[1][2] By utilizing a patch-based delivery system cleared through the 510(k) pathway, MimiX aims to expedite the application of advanced wound management solutions, reflecting a shift toward "smart" dressings that facilitate faster, more efficient patient recovery outside of traditional surgical settings.[1][2]
Regulatory Observations (2025–2026)
During this period, the FDA has heavily emphasized Computer Software Assurance (CSA), a risk-based approach to software validation that encourages manufacturers to focus testing on areas with the highest impact on patient safety.[1][2][5] Additionally, the mandatory transition to eSTAR (electronic Submission Template and Resource) has successfully standardized submissions, while the new Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR)—aligning FDA requirements with ISO 13485:2016—has forced manufacturers to modernize their internal quality systems.[1][2]
